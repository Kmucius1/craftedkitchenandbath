> **Quick answer:** In Tampa Bay, most kitchen remodels take about **6 to 12 weeks of on-site construction**, and most bathroom remodels take about **3 to 6 weeks**. Counting design, product ordering, and permits, the full project usually runs **3 to 6 months for a kitchen** and **2 to 4 months for a bathroom**, from the first consultation to the final walkthrough. Cabinet lead times, layout changes, and permit review are the three things that move the timeline the most.

"How long will this take?" is usually the second question homeowners ask us, right after "How much will it cost?"

It matters because a remodel affects daily life. A kitchen remodel can mean weeks of eating takeout. A bathroom remodel can mean sharing one bathroom with the whole family. Knowing the real timeline up front helps you plan around holidays, guests, travel, and work, and it keeps you from getting frustrated by delays that were predictable all along.

At **Crafted Kitchen & Bath**, we plan and build kitchen and bathroom remodels for homeowners across **Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Pasco counties**. This guide walks through each phase of a remodel, how long each one usually takes, and what you can do to keep your project on schedule.

## Kitchen and Bathroom Remodel Timelines at a Glance

These are typical ranges for Tampa Bay projects. Your timeline depends on the size of the space, the products you choose, and how much of the layout changes.

**Kitchen remodel**

- Design and selections: 2 to 6 weeks
- Product lead times (cabinets, counters, special orders): 2 to 12+ weeks
- Permits, when required: a few days to several weeks
- On-site construction: 6 to 12 weeks
- **Total from first meeting to finished kitchen: about 3 to 6 months**

**Bathroom remodel**

- Design and selections: 1 to 4 weeks
- Product lead times (vanity, tile, glass, fixtures): 1 to 8 weeks
- Permits, when required: a few days to several weeks
- On-site construction: 3 to 6 weeks
- **Total from first meeting to finished bathroom: about 2 to 4 months**

**Smaller projects move faster.** A countertop replacement, a backsplash, or cabinet refacing can often be finished in days to a couple of weeks of on-site work once materials are ready. A full interior remodel that includes a kitchen, bathrooms, and flooring takes longer and is usually scheduled in phases.

Many design and ordering steps overlap, so the phases do not simply add up. The construction clock is what most homeowners feel, because that is when the room is out of service.

## Phase 1: Consultation and Measurements (About 1 to 2 Weeks)

Every project starts with a conversation. We visit your home, measure the space, and talk through how you use the room, what frustrates you about it now, and what your budget looks like. You are also welcome to visit our **Oldsmar showroom** to see materials in person.

This phase is short, but it shapes everything that follows. A clear scope at the start prevents the change orders that cause delays later.

## Phase 2: Design and Selections (About 2 to 6 Weeks)

This is where the plan becomes real. Layout, cabinetry, countertops, tile, flooring, plumbing fixtures, lighting, and hardware all get chosen and approved.

The biggest factor here is decision speed. Homeowners who know what they want can finish selections in a couple of weeks. Homeowners who want to compare many options take longer, and that is fine, as long as it happens **before** demolition and not during it.

Ways to move faster in this phase:

- Save photos of kitchens or bathrooms you like and note what you like about each one
- Decide your non-negotiables early (for example, a larger shower or a kitchen island)
- Agree on a budget range before you start shopping finishes
- Make every selection before construction starts, not after

If you are not sure where to begin, our guide on [how to plan a kitchen or bathroom remodel without feeling overwhelmed](https://craftedkitchenandbath.com/blog/how-to-plan-kitchen-bathroom-remodel) is a good starting point.

## Phase 3: Ordering and Lead Times (About 2 to 12+ Weeks)

Once selections are final, materials are ordered. This is often the longest wait in the whole project, and it happens before anyone touches your existing kitchen or bathroom.

Typical lead times look like this:

- **Stock cabinets:** about 1 to 3 weeks
- **Semi-custom cabinets:** about 4 to 8 weeks
- **Custom cabinets:** about 8 to 12 weeks or more
- **Quartz, granite, or quartzite countertops:** fabricated after the cabinets are installed and templated, usually 1 to 2 weeks from template to install
- **Special-order tile, plumbing fixtures, and glass shower enclosures:** a few days to several weeks, depending on the product

Cabinets usually set the pace for a kitchen. For a bathroom, special-order vanities, tile, and custom shower glass are usually the long-lead items.

**Our approach:** we prefer to have key materials on hand or confirmed before demolition. That way your kitchen or bathroom is not torn apart while everyone waits on a delayed shipment.

## Phase 4: Permits (A Few Days to Several Weeks)

Not every remodel needs a permit. Purely cosmetic work, such as paint or swapping a faucet in the same location, often does not. Work that changes plumbing, electrical, gas lines, or walls usually does.

Permit timing in Tampa Bay depends on the city or county reviewing the application. Oldsmar, Clearwater, St. Petersburg, Tampa, and unincorporated Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Pasco counties each run their own process. Simple permits can be issued quickly, while projects that need plan review take longer.

Florida law also puts limits on how long many residential permit reviews can take. Under [Section 553.792 of the Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/553.792), local governments generally must approve, approve with conditions, or deny a complete permit application for a single-family home under 7,500 square feet within 30 business days. That is a maximum, not the typical wait.

As a licensed Florida contractor (**CGC1541816**), we coordinate permits where required and build the permit schedule into your project timeline, so it does not come as a surprise.

## Phase 5: Construction (Kitchen: 6 to 12 Weeks, Bathroom: 3 to 6 Weeks)

This is the part most homeowners picture when they think about a remodel. The order of work usually looks like this:

1. **Protection and demolition** (1 to 5 days): floors and nearby rooms are protected, then old cabinets, counters, tile, and fixtures come out.
2. **Rough-in work** (about 1 to 2 weeks): plumbing, electrical, and any framing changes. Moving a sink, adding outlets, or relocating a shower happens here.
3. **Inspections** (a few days): when a permit is open, rough-in work is inspected before walls are closed.
4. **Drywall and prep** (about 1 week): walls are closed, patched, and prepared for finishes.
5. **Cabinets, tile, and flooring** (about 1 to 3 weeks): cabinets go in, then shower walls, backsplash, and floors.
6. **Countertops** (about 1 to 2 weeks from template): stone counters are measured on top of the installed cabinets, fabricated, and installed.
7. **Fixtures, hardware, and finishes** (about 1 week): faucets, lighting, appliances, hardware, shower glass, and paint touch-ups.
8. **Final inspection and walkthrough** (a few days): we walk the finished space with you and take care of every detail.

Bathrooms follow the same order on a smaller scale. Shower construction adds time because waterproofing and tile must be done carefully and allowed to cure properly. Rushing this step is how leaks happen.

## What Causes Remodeling Delays?

Most delays are predictable. Here are the most common ones we see in Tampa Bay homes:

- **Changing selections mid-project.** Swapping a tile or countertop after it is ordered restarts the lead-time clock.
- **Hidden conditions behind the walls.** Older homes can have outdated wiring, corroded plumbing, subfloor damage, or past water damage that must be fixed before work continues.
- **Moisture and water damage.** Florida humidity and slow leaks under sinks and around showers can damage subfloors and framing, which is not visible until demolition.
- **Back-ordered products.** A single missing item, such as a specific faucet or cabinet panel, can hold up the final stage.
- **Permit corrections.** If a reviewer asks for changes, the applicant has a set window to respond, and the review time restarts.
- **Layout changes.** Moving plumbing, gas, or walls adds trade work and inspections.
- **Hurricane season and holidays.** Storms and holiday weeks can slow deliveries, inspections, and trade schedules.

## How to Keep Your Remodel on Schedule

You cannot control everything, but you can control a lot. These steps make the biggest difference:

- **Finish all selections before demolition.** This is the single best way to protect your timeline.
- **Order long-lead items early.** Cabinets and special-order products should be confirmed before the room is torn out.
- **Build a contingency into your budget.** Setting aside about 10 to 15 percent for hidden conditions keeps surprises from stalling the job. Our [Tampa Bay kitchen remodel cost guide](https://craftedkitchenandbath.com/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost-tampa-bay) explains how to budget for this.
- **Keep the layout where it makes sense.** If the current layout works, keeping plumbing in place saves time and money. If it does not work, change it now rather than living with it for 20 years.
- **Pick one point of contact.** Clear communication between you and your contractor prevents misunderstandings and rework.
- **Plan around your calendar.** If you want the kitchen done before Thanksgiving or family visits, start the design phase several months earlier.

## Can You Live at Home During a Remodel?

Usually, yes. Most homeowners stay home during a kitchen or bathroom remodel.

During a kitchen remodel, many families set up a temporary kitchen in another room with a microwave, coffee maker, refrigerator, and a folding table. Planning simple meals and using disposable plates for a few weeks helps a lot.

During a bathroom remodel, living at home is easy if there is another working bathroom. If it is the only bathroom in the home, talk with your contractor about scheduling so the room is out of service for as little time as possible.

We keep job sites clean and protected, and we schedule work around your household so the disruption stays manageable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a kitchen remodel take in Tampa Bay?

Most kitchen remodels in Tampa Bay take about 6 to 12 weeks of on-site construction. Including design, ordering, and permits, the full project usually takes about 3 to 6 months from the first consultation to the finished kitchen.

### How long does a bathroom remodel take?

Most bathroom remodels take about 3 to 6 weeks of on-site construction. Including design, ordering, and permits, the full project usually takes about 2 to 4 months. A simple update that keeps the existing layout can be faster.

### What takes the longest in a kitchen remodel?

Cabinet lead times usually take the longest. Semi-custom cabinets often take about 4 to 8 weeks to arrive, and custom cabinets can take 8 to 12 weeks or more. Countertops are fabricated after the cabinets are installed, which adds about 1 to 2 weeks.

### Do I need a permit for a kitchen or bathroom remodel in Florida?

It depends on the scope. Cosmetic updates often do not need a permit. Work that changes plumbing, electrical, gas lines, or walls usually does. Rules vary by city and county, so a licensed contractor should confirm permit requirements for your address.

### How long does it take to get a remodeling permit in Pinellas or Hillsborough County?

Simple permits can be issued in a few days, while permits that need plan review can take several weeks. Florida law generally requires local governments to act on a complete single-family residential permit application within 30 business days for homes under 7,500 square feet.

### Can I live in my house during a kitchen remodel?

Yes, most homeowners stay home during a kitchen remodel. Setting up a temporary kitchen in another room with a microwave, coffee maker, and refrigerator makes the construction weeks much easier.

### What is the fastest way to update a kitchen?

The fastest kitchen updates keep the existing layout. Cabinet refacing, new countertops, a new backsplash, updated lighting, and new hardware can change the look of a kitchen in much less time than a full remodel.

### Why do remodels go over schedule?

The most common causes are changing selections after ordering, hidden damage behind walls, back-ordered products, permit corrections, and layout changes made during construction. Finishing selections before demolition prevents most delays.

### When should I start planning a remodel to finish before the holidays?

Start the design phase about 4 to 6 months before you want a kitchen finished, and about 3 to 4 months ahead for a bathroom. That leaves time for selections, cabinet lead times, permits, and construction.
